# The Outdoor Tent Checklist: Plan for Wind Before the Wind Plans for You

An outdoor reception or event can be spoiled by one gust. Tents that are too small, too light or poorly anchored are one of the most common avoidable problems on outdoor event days, and most of them can be solved in planning. Here is a checklist you can use with clients and suppliers.

**Start with the site.** Ask what the ground is: grass, gravel, pavement or deck. Stakes work in soil but are often banned on pavement and anywhere underground utilities run, so many venues require weights instead. Confirm access for delivery, a flat footprint, and where power and cables will cross the tent entrance.

**Size for the guests, then add margin.** Count seated guests, service lines, dance floor and any bar or buffet, then leave room for walkways inside and outside the tent. Frame tents, pole tents and pop-up canopies serve different jobs: pop-ups suit vendor tables, welcome stations and small lounge spaces, while larger structures call for a professional tent installer.

**Plan the weights early.** Published event rules show how much weight is expected. Common minimums are around 24 to 25 lb per leg, 40 lb per leg is typical for many craft and vendor events, and large art fairs often ask for 50 to 60 lb per leg. Rules for guest tents at venues vary, so ask the venue and the supplier for their requirement in writing. Sandbags, cast-iron leg weights and concrete-filled pipes are common options. Weights should be fastened to the leg, not just placed nearby, and kept low so guests do not trip on them.

**Treat walls like sails.** Side walls and back walls add shelter and branding space, but they also add wind load. Add weight whenever walls go up, open or remove walls if gusts rise, and agree on who makes that call on the day.

**Check fire and venue rules.** Tents used with heaters, cooking or lighting often need flame-retardant fabric, commonly certified to NFPA 701 in the US. Some venues and municipalities require certificates and inspections. Ask early, because paperwork can take time.

**Choose the frame as carefully as the fabric.** A commercial-grade aluminum or steel frame performs far better in wind than a consumer pop-up. Check the rated wind speed, ask what happens above it, and plan to take the structure down if conditions pass that limit.

**Decide your branding or decor.** Printed or decorated canopy tops, walls and table covers can carry a couple's colors, a sponsor's logo or wayfinding. Color and logo rules vary by venue and event, and some require plain white tops. Allow time for an artwork proof and production, and order well before the date.

**Build a wind plan.** Name a person responsible, set a trigger for lowering walls and a trigger for evacuating the tent, and keep a plan B indoors or under a hard structure. Keep emergency contacts for the venue and the supplier.

**Questions to ask any tent supplier.** What are the dimensions and the rated wind speed? What weights or anchors are required, and who supplies them? Is the fabric flame-retardant, and is a certificate available? What is the lead time, and when is the artwork deadline? Who will be on site, and what is the cancellation policy?

A good tent plan is invisible on the day. Ask these questions early, and the wind becomes just another guest.
