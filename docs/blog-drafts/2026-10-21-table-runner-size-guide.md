# Table Runner Size Guide: How to Choose the Right Length and Width for Your Trade Show Table

**Match the runner length to your table length, or add 6 to 12 inches at each end if you want it to hang over. Make the width about one third of the table's depth.** For a standard 6ft table that means a 72-inch runner flush, or 84 to 96 inches with overhang. For an 8ft table it means 96 inches flush, or 108 to 120 inches with overhang. This table runner size guide covers the numbers, the exceptions, and the mistakes we see at setup.

We are a Vancouver team of 15 (industrial printers, marketers and brand builders) with 12 years in printing, and across 900+ booth setups the most common sizing problem is not a wrong measurement. It is a measurement taken from memory. Tables are never the size you remember.

## Standard Table Runner Sizes: Inches and CM

A table runner is a long, narrow strip laid along the top of a table. Unlike a full cover, it leaves the edges visible. Here is the size chart for the tables you will actually meet at shows.

### Table Runner Size Chart for Common Trade Show Tables

| Table length | Flush runner (inches / cm) | With 6 to 12 in overhang each end (inches / cm) |
|---|---|---|
| 4 ft | 48 in / 122 cm | 60 to 72 in / 152 to 183 cm |
| 6 ft | 72 in / 183 cm | 84 to 96 in / 213 to 244 cm |
| 8 ft | 96 in / 244 cm | 108 to 120 in / 274 to 305 cm |

These are the arithmetic: table length, plus the overhang on both ends. One inch is 2.54 cm, so you can convert any custom length yourself.

### Table Runner Dimensions in Inches

Length is set by the table; width is your design choice. Our [table runner options](/products/custom-table-runner) come in 72" and 96" lengths (6ft and 8ft tables) or a custom length, in 14", 18", 24" and 30" widths or a custom width.

### Table Runner Sizes in CM

If your show or supplier works in metric: 14 in is 36 cm, 18 in is 46 cm, 24 in is 61 cm and 30 in is 76 cm. A 72 in runner is 183 cm and a 96 in runner is 244 cm. Always give your printer the final dimensions in one unit, and say which one.

## How Long Should a Table Runner Be?

There are two valid answers, and they suit different jobs.

- **Flush (same length as the table):** clean, and nothing hangs where people walk. This is a common choice for trade show tables because the end cannot snag.
- **Overhang (6 to 12 inches per end):** the classic dining look. It reads as deliberate and gives you more room for a logo that wraps the table end.

If you are unsure, go flush. A runner that is slightly short looks like a decision; one that is too long looks like a mistake that drags through the aisle.

## What Size Table Runner for a 6ft Table?

A 6ft table is 72 inches (183 cm) long, so a 72-inch runner sits flush. If you want overhang, choose 84 to 96 inches (213 to 244 cm). Typical rental tables of this size are about 30 inches deep, but depth varies by venue, so measure or ask. Most 6ft trade show setups are the one table at the front of a 10x10 booth.

## What Size Table Runner for an 8ft Table?

An 8ft table is 96 inches (244 cm) long, so a 96-inch runner sits flush. For overhang, use 108 to 120 inches (274 to 305 cm). The longer table gives a logo more room to repeat, but it also shows any crease or off-centre placement more clearly. Check the runner is centred before you set out your products.

## Standard Table Runner Width: What to Know

Most runners are 14 to 22 inches wide (36 to 56 cm). A reliable rule is to make the width about one third of the table depth. On a 30-inch deep table that is roughly 10 inches by the rule, but at trade shows a wider band reads better from the aisle, so 14 to 24 inches is typical.

Widths to think about:

- **14 in (36 cm):** a slim accent; best when products cover most of the table.
- **18 in (46 cm):** a balanced default for brochure holders and a laptop.
- **24 in (61 cm):** a strong brand band; leaves a narrow strip of table each side.
- **30 in (76 cm):** nearly the full depth of many tables; at this point compare it with a cover.

## How to Measure Your Table for a Table Runner

1. Measure the table top **length** and **depth**, not the legs or the booth.
2. Decide flush or overhang. Add the overhang to both ends.
3. Choose a width using the one-third rule, then adjust for what will sit on it.
4. Ask the show's exhibitor services which table you are getting. Rental tables are not always the size on the listing.
5. Send the final length and width, in one unit, with your artwork.

## Table Runner vs. Table Cover: What's the Difference?

A runner is a strip down the middle; a cover goes over the whole table and hangs down the sides. Choose a runner to add a branded accent and keep the table visible. Choose a cover to hide storage under the table and brand the front. On the question of table runner vs tablecloth: a tablecloth is a cover. It protects and conceals the whole surface, while a runner decorates part of it.

## Trade Show Table Covers: Choosing the Right Runner Size

Trade show table covers and runners are often used together. The cover sets the table length; the runner sits on top and should be shorter, or equal, never longer than the cover. If both carry a logo, keep the runner's width narrower than the cover's logo area so they do not compete. Browse the full range of [table covers](/table-covers).

## Custom Table Covers and Table Runners: How They Work Together

Custom table covers set the base colour and the front-of-table logo; a runner adds a second band of branding on the top. A simple approach is a solid or lightly patterned cover with a runner that carries the logo. Order both to the same table measurement so the lengths line up.

## Table Cover with Logo: Sizing Considerations for Runners

If your cover already has a logo on the front panel, keep the runner logo smaller or place it once at the end that faces the aisle. A table cover with logo on the front and a repeated logo on a runner can look busy. Pick one to lead.

## Custom Tablecloth with Logo: Size Guide for Runners

A custom tablecloth with logo is the same as a cover for sizing purposes: it is built to the table length and drop. When you add a runner, leave at least a hand's width of cloth visible on each side, or the two read as one blurry block. Wider than 24 inches on a 30-inch table is the point where we usually suggest looking at a cover alone.

## A Custom Table Cover Instead? When a Runner Is Not Enough

A custom table cover is the better choice when you store boxes under the table, when the venue supplies a scruffy table, or when you want the front of the table to carry your name. A runner can't hide any of those. It is the light option, not the complete one.

## FAQ

### What size table runner do I need for a 6ft table?
A 72-inch (183 cm) runner sits flush on a 6ft table. For a 6 to 12 inch overhang at each end, use 84 to 96 inches (213 to 244 cm).

### How long should a table runner be for an 8ft table?
A 96-inch (244 cm) runner sits flush on an 8ft table. For overhang, use 108 to 120 inches (274 to 305 cm).

### What is the standard width of a table runner?
Most runners are 14 to 22 inches (36 to 56 cm) wide, and about one third of the table depth is a good starting point. For trade show tables, 14, 18, 24 and 30 inch widths are the common choices.

### Should a table runner hang over the edge of the table?
At the ends, a 6 to 12 inch overhang looks deliberate. At trade shows, flush is also common because nothing hangs into the aisle.

### How do I measure for a table runner?
Measure the table top length and depth, add the overhang you want to both ends, then choose a width of about one third of the depth. Confirm the table with the show's exhibitor services first.

### What is the difference between a table runner and a tablecloth?
A runner is a strip along the top that leaves the edges visible. A tablecloth or cover goes over the entire table and can close off the front and sides.
